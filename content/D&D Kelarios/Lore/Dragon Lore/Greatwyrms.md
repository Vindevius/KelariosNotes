**Greatwyrms** are a rare and powerful subset of dragons in Dungeons & Dragons, surpassing even ancient dragons in terms of age, power, and influence. Given their extreme rarity and the scale of their abilities, the number of greatwyrms in a world is typically very low.

### Factors Affecting the Number of Greatwyrms:
1. **Age and Longevity**: Greatwyrms are over **1,000 years old**, which means they represent an extremely long-lived and rarified category of dragons. Most dragons, even ancient ones, do not live long enough to reach this stage.
2. **Power and Influence**: Greatwyrms are among the most powerful creatures in the world. Their powers are so vast that their existence often defines the course of entire civilizations or regions. Having too many greatwyrms would drastically unbalance the world, as they could dominate both mortal societies and other dragons.
3. **Rarity**: Given their extreme age and power, greatwyrms are considered mythic or legendary beings, often treated as forces of nature. Their appearance is rare and usually tied to powerful events or epic conflicts.

### Recommended Number of Greatwyrms:
- **1–3 per major continent or region**: In most campaign settings, a **world might only have between 3 to 5 greatwyrms total**, often spread across different continents or regions. Greatwyrms are unique beings that are deeply entrenched in the lore of the world, often immortalized in myth and legend. Their presence is a defining feature of the world, and there would generally only be a handful to ensure they remain rare and imposing.
- **10–20 across the entire world**: If you have a very large world or a setting with many powerful dragons, you might push the number up to **10–20** greatwyrms, though they should still be considered the top of the draconic hierarchy. Even with this number, greatwyrms would be treated as god-like figures, shaping events in the world for centuries or even millennia.

### Key Points to Consider:
- **Isolated Legends**: Greatwyrms are often solitary or part of very small groups. They don’t typically form large societies or hoards, as their power is so immense that they rarely need to work with others. Each greatwyrm is often tied to a specific, significant place in the world.
- **World Balance**: If there are too many greatwyrms in a setting, their power could overshadow the rest of the world, and the adventurers and other factions might seem insignificant in comparison. Keeping the number low helps preserve their mystique and the threat they represent.
- **Role in the Story**: Greatwyrms are often central to world-altering events, such as ancient prophecies, cataclysmic battles, or significant political upheaval. Too many would dilute their impact, while a few carefully placed greatwyrms can become monumental forces that drive major plot points.

### Conclusion:
In a typical D&D world, **3 to 5 greatwyrms** is a reasonable number to maintain the balance of power and the sense of awe around these legendary creatures. If you wish to have more for a particularly epic or sprawling world, you might have **10 to 20**, but they should remain unique and very influential figures in the world.

# Greatwyrms Locations

Greatwyrms, as legendary and god-like dragons, are typically found in **remote, isolated, or dangerous locations** that suit their immense power and age. Their lairs are often places of great significance, both geographically and culturally, where they can remain undisturbed for centuries. These locations are typically not only difficult to reach, but also represent key points of magical, historical, or territorial importance. Here are a few examples of where greatwyrms might be found:

### 1. **Ancient Ruins or Lost Civilizations**
Greatwyrms might reside in the **ruins of ancient civilizations** that have long since fallen into obscurity. These places could be home to vast treasure hoards, magical artifacts, and forgotten lore. The ruins may be difficult to reach or have been deliberately hidden to protect the wyrm’s lair from unwanted attention.
- **Example**: A greatwyrm might be the last guardian of the **ruins of an ancient empire**, keeping watch over a city once ruled by dragons or other powerful creatures, now lost to time.

### 2. **Mountains, Volcanoes, and Remote Peaks**
Many greatwyrms prefer high, inhospitable places like **mountain peaks** or **volcanic craters**, where they can perch in isolation and survey vast territories. These areas provide natural defenses and the potential for hidden caves or lairs. A wyrm in such a place may be the source of the region’s natural disasters or volcanic activity.
- **Example**: A **fire dragon greatwyrm** could live deep within a volcano, its power fueling eruptions and casting a shadow over surrounding towns. Alternatively, an **ice dragon greatwyrm** could dwell atop a glacial peak, surrounded by eternal snow and ice.

### 3. **Caverns Deep Beneath the Earth**
Some greatwyrms choose to reside deep underground in the **underdark** or within **forgotten caverns** beneath the earth. These regions are often labyrinthine, filled with dangerous creatures, and largely unexplored by surface dwellers. A greatwyrm might have carved out its lair in the heart of a vast, ancient cave system, surrounded by mineral veins and strange subterranean flora and fauna.
- **Example**: A **shadow dragon greatwyrm** could reside in the deepest recesses of the **Underdark**, in a cavern filled with strange magical energy that warps the shadows around it, making it nearly impossible for intruders to find or survive.

### 4. **Forgotten or Sacred Forests**
Greatwyrms, especially those of more **naturalistic or neutral alignments**, might be found in **ancient forests** or **sacred groves**. These forests are typically untouched by civilization and might even be considered sacred or magical. The greatwyrm may be the guardian of the land, protecting it from corruption or exploitation.
- **Example**: A **forest dragon greatwyrm** might reside in a mystical **forest** where the trees are ancient and imbued with arcane energies, or perhaps a **green dragon greatwyrm** guards a forest that is the last remnant of a dying species of plant or animal.

### 5. **Islands and Oceanic Depths**
Greatwyrms that are **aquatic** or have the ability to live in the sea may make their homes in **deep ocean trenches**, **forgotten islands**, or **sunken cities** beneath the water. These areas are often unreachable by conventional means, and the isolation from the surface world ensures the wyrm’s seclusion.
- **Example**: A **sea dragon greatwyrm** could dwell beneath the waves, in an ancient city lost to the sea, its lair surrounded by powerful currents, shipwrecks, and aquatic monsters that serve as its guardians.

### 6. **Planes of Existence (Astral, Elemental, or Shadow Realms)**
Some greatwyrms may have transcended the material plane and dwell in one of the **outer planes** of existence. They could be embodiments of the elements, powers of the cosmos, or creatures that are bound to specific domains like the **Shadowfell**, the **Elemental Plane of Fire**, or the **Astral Plane**. These locations are often far removed from the mortal realm and may only be accessible through specific portals, rituals, or magical means.
- **Example**: A **storm dragon greatwyrm** could be the guardian of a rift between the **Material Plane** and the **Elemental Plane of Air**, existing in a sky-temple that floats between the realms, constantly shifting with winds and clouds.

### 7. **Magical Nexus or Power Source**
Greatwyrms are often tied to **magical locations** where powerful energies converge. These places are often filled with arcane or divine power and may be the source of certain magical phenomena. The wyrm may guard these places for centuries, ensuring that no one corrupts or exploits the magic.
- **Example**: A **lunar dragon greatwyrm** might dwell in a **moonlit cave** or **magical nexus** where the flow of arcane energy is strongest, or perhaps a **copper dragon greatwyrm** is the guardian of a **magical wellspring** that gives life to the land or powers ancient enchantments.

### 8. **A Stronghold or Kingdom**
In some cases, greatwyrms may have established **personal kingdoms** or **territories** that they rule over with great authority. These areas are typically surrounded by vast hordes of lesser dragons, drakes, or dragon-like creatures who serve the greatwyrm in various ways.
- **Example**: An **ancient gold dragon greatwyrm** might have established a **draconic kingdom** within a mountain range, where it rules over a small enclave of dragons and other creatures loyal to it.

### Summary of Typical Greatwyrm Locations:
1. **Ancient Ruins**: Forgotten civilizations or lost cities that are the site of untold treasure and lore.
2. **Mountains or Volcanoes**: High, difficult-to-reach peaks and active volcanic regions.
3. **Underground Caverns**: Vast subterranean cave systems deep beneath the earth.
4. **Sacred Forests**: Magical or ancient forests that hold arcane or divine significance.
5. **Islands or Oceanic Depths**: Sunken cities, ocean trenches, or forgotten islands beneath the sea.
6. **Outer Planes**: The Shadowfell, Elemental Planes, or Astral Plane, where they may transcend physical form.
7. **Magical Nexus**: Locations with immense arcane power, such as ley lines or wellsprings of magic.
8. **Strongholds or Kingdoms**: Personal lairs that serve as a dragon's throne and domain, often with vast hoards.

### Final Thought:
Greatwyrms are rarely found in places easily accessible by adventurers. Their lairs are usually located in **remote, highly guarded, or magical locations**, often serving as a testament to their power and importance in the world. Their lairs are also key points in the world’s lore, shaping history and the balance of power within their regions.
