In D&D, dragons are traditionally divided into three main types: **Chromatic Dragons** (typically evil), **Metallic Dragons** (typically good), and **Gem Dragons** (typically neutral and often associated with psionics). Here’s a list of the most common dragons in each category:

### 1. **Chromatic Dragons**

- **Black Dragon**: Acid-breathing, swamp-dwelling dragons known for their cruelty and cunning.
- **Blue Dragon**: Lightning-breathing, desert-dwelling dragons that are highly territorial and prideful.
- **Green Dragon**: Poison-breathing, forest-dwelling dragons skilled in manipulation and deception.
- **Red Dragon**: Fire-breathing, mountain-dwelling dragons with a love for treasure and conquest.
- **White Dragon**: Ice-breathing, arctic-dwelling dragons that are more animalistic and feral.

### 2. **Metallic Dragons**

- **Brass Dragon**: Fire-breathing, desert-dwelling dragons known for their talkative and friendly nature.
- **Bronze Dragon**: Lightning-breathing, coastal-dwelling dragons that are honorable and warlike.
- **Copper Dragon**: Acid-breathing, rocky terrain-dwelling dragons known for their wit and love of pranks.
- **Gold Dragon**: Fire (or weakening gas)-breathing, mountain-dwelling dragons that are noble and wise.
- **Silver Dragon**: Cold-breathing, alpine-dwelling dragons that are kind, protective, and enjoy the company of humanoids.

### 3. **Gem Dragons**

- **Amethyst Dragon**: Force-breathing dragons associated with balance, known for their calm and contemplative nature.
- **Crystal Dragon**: Radiant-breathing dragons that are curious, playful, and live in arctic environments.
- **Emerald Dragon**: Psychic-breathing dragons that are reclusive, guarding against threats to their knowledge.
- **Sapphire Dragon**: Thunder-breathing dragons that are warlike, preferring the Underdark as their home.
- **Topaz Dragon**: Necrotic-breathing dragons that are aloof and prefer desolate coastlines.

These dragons have unique personalities, abilities, and traits that make them distinct, reflecting the nature of their type and environment.
