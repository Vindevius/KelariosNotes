- ### The Blood War: Baator vs the Abyss
	- Those who die and come here become a larva or a mane. They can then rise up to become a devil or demon and become part of the blood war. That is why devils and demons seek to corrupt mortals. Most of the blood war takes place in the Gray Waste (Oinos). Recently, the battle has shifted to Carceri when Asmodeus became a chief deity replacing Domexius as ruler of Carceri. Zehir directly opposes Asmodeus.

### 1. **Ideological Conflict: Order vs. Destruction**

- **Purpose**: The devils, now embodying order rather than lawful evil, would seek to enforce structure and stability across the multiverse, seeing themselves as the necessary architects of cosmic balance. The demons, embodying destruction rather than chaotic evil, would strive to tear down what they see as restrictive or stagnant, believing that destruction is essential for rebirth and change.
- **Motivation**: Devils would fight to impose their vision of structured existence, while demons would aim to break down these structures to allow new forms of existence to emerge. Rather than a simple struggle between good and evil, it’s now about whether order or chaos holds the ultimate power to shape the cosmos.

### 2. **Broader Impact Across the Planes**

- **Recruitment and Influence**: With these ideals at the forefront, the Blood War would have wider-reaching alliances across the planes. Creatures, mortals, and gods from various realms might align with either side based on personal philosophies and needs, not just moral alignment. An Archon from Arcadia might ally with devils to preserve order, while a primal spirit from the Beastlands might support demons to foster natural cycles of destruction and rebirth.
- **Realm Involvement**: Other realms could feel pressured to take sides. For instance, Mechanus (Realm of Precision) might become an ally to Baator, seeing the devils’ order as necessary to maintain universal stability. Meanwhile, the Abyss (Realm of Destruction) could attract forces from Ysgard (Realm of Valor), where warriors might see the constant chaos as a chance for endless challenges and battle.

### 3. **Strategic Changes**

- **Shifting Tactics**: Devils might focus on strengthening their strongholds, enacting treaties, and solidifying control over key planes or portals, working to bring an orderly “unity” to the multiverse. Demons, in contrast, would use guerilla tactics, spontaneous assaults, and chaotic incursions to destabilize this order, forcing the devils to constantly adapt.
- **New Battlefronts**: Rather than a confined battle in the Lower Planes, the Blood War could expand to places like the Outlands (as a neutral ground) and Mechanus (as a realm valuable to both ideals). Clashes over ideological influence could bring the fight to planar trade routes, celestial gatherings, or the borders of planes where order and destruction are in natural tension.

### 4. **Role of Asmodeus and Demon Lords**

- **Asmodeus**: Now a Lord of Order, Asmodeus would see himself as the champion of cosmic structure, often acting less as a tyrant and more as a ruler focused on preventing the multiverse from descending into chaos. His tactics might include using contracts, alliances, and manipulation of other planes to strengthen the influence of order across realms, positioning himself as a counterbalance to the Abyss's destruction.
- **Demon Lords**: Figures like Demogorgon or Orcus would embody facets of destruction, such as decay, fear, and primal chaos. Their motivations might not be purely destructive but could reflect a drive to expose the “rotting foundations” of structures they see as corrupt or stagnant. They may even philosophically justify destruction as the “necessary purging” for life to evolve, putting them in ideological opposition to the devils.

### 5. **Expanded Role of Mortal Involvement**

- **Mortals Choosing Sides**: Without alignment dictating loyalties, mortals from different worlds might be drawn into the Blood War based on their ideals. Wizards, soldiers, and adventurers could find themselves choosing between the devils’ promise of order and the demons’ allure of chaotic freedom, depending on what resonates with their own beliefs and societies.
- **Neutral Forces**: Mortals and planar factions might band together to attempt peace treaties, arbitrations, or temporary truces in order to avoid the devastating impact of the Blood War spilling into other realms. Sigil and the Outlands could become neutral meeting grounds for such discussions, as planes and factions seek ways to manage the escalating conflict without choosing sides.

### 6. **Complex Moral Implications**

- **Shades of Gray**: This version of the Blood War would present less clear-cut villains and heroes, as both sides could claim to be acting in the multiverse’s best interest. The devils could argue that unchecked chaos would lead to collapse, while the demons might contend that without destruction, there is no space for growth or freedom. Adventurers might encounter both sympathetic devils trying to uphold cosmic balance and “noble” demons seeking freedom from an overly controlled universe.

### 7. **The War’s Endgame and Consequences**

- **Ideological Victory**: A victory by either side could lead to starkly different multiverses. A triumph of the devils would see a rigidly ordered universe, where every realm’s function and purpose are dictated by a larger design, possibly stifling innovation and freedom. If the demons win, the multiverse might be in constant flux, where destruction brings creation but also continuous instability, potentially unmaking realms and structures in its wake.
- **Perpetual Stalemate**: The war could be a never-ending clash of ideals, with neither side capable of a total victory. The multiverse might come to rely on this balance, where order and destruction are in perpetual conflict, each providing the balance necessary to allow growth and decay in equal measure.

By shifting the Blood War’s focus from alignment to ideals, this reimagining would provide a richer, more morally complex backdrop. Players and characters would be forced to consider the costs and benefits of both order and destruction, and even the most stalwart hero or villain might find themselves aligning with forces that share their personal ideals, regardless of their past affiliations.
